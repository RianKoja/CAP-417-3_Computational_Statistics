## Exercise requirements to satisfy

The original exercise requires investigation of time series whose PDFs display **multimodality, skewness, heavy tails, and mixtures of generating processes**. It explicitly asks for comparison of generating multimodal series, fitting PDFs with KDE, and locating fluctuation patterns in the Cullen-Frey-Pearson space. It also explicitly requires a **benchmark between two agents**.[file:4][file:1]

The exercise is divided into four parts:

### Part 3.1 — Generation of multimodal series
The work must show algorithms capable of generating PDFs that are **bimodal**, **multimodal**, and **asymmetric**, while allowing control over the **number of modes**, **overlap between modes**, **different weights between modes**, and **non-Gaussian modes** such as Gaussian, skewed, Laplace, and others.[file:4][file:1]

### Part 3.2 — Histogram vs KDE
The work must compare a **traditional histogram** and **KDE**, investigate the **effect of bandwidth**, **over-smoothing**, and **overfitting**, and explicitly answer whether the histogram represents the PDF adequately, when KDE improves modeling, how bandwidth changes the result, and whether KDE can detect multimodality.[file:4][file:1]

### Part 3.3 — Cullen-Frey-Pearson space
The work must project the generated signals into the Cullen-Frey-Pearson space with **x-axis = skewness²** and **y-axis = kurtosis**, using **multiple realizations** of each type and plotting **Gaussian**, **multimodal**, **asymmetric**, and **heavy-tailed** signals.[file:4][file:1]

### Part 3.4 — Conceptual discussion
The work must answer: where multimodal signals fall in the Cullen-Frey space; whether multimodal mixtures occupy classical regions; whether different distributions can fall close together; whether Cullen-Frey detects multimodality; and whether KDE and Cullen-Frey provide complementary information.[file:4][file:1]

### Benchmark requirement
The notebook must compare the two agents in terms of **generation strategy**, **KDE implementation**, **bandwidth approach**, **Cullen-Frey visual results**, **code organization clarity**, and **robustness / numerical stability**.[file:4][file:1]

## Quality bar for the benchmark analysis

The comparison must be technically serious.

- One agent uses clearer bandwidth contrasts, making over-smoothing more visually obvious.

- One agent provides more direct evidence for overlap control or weight imbalance.
- One agent's outputs are complete but visually weaker; the other is visually strong but incomplete.

- “Both are good.”
- “The results seem similar.”
- “Claude is better” without evidence.
- “Gemini is more robust” without pointing to actual output completeness or consistency.

Every comparative claim must be supported by visible outputs or readable statistics.
